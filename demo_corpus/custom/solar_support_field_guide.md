# Coastal Solar Support Field Guide - 50 Incident Cards

This corpus contains fifty compact field cards for a retrieval demo about solar
panel mounting systems in marine and industrial environments.

## Card 01 - Mist-polished rail
Coastal mornings can leave a rail looking clean while a chloride film remains on
the underside. Inspect the shaded lip before approving the clamp torque.

## Card 02 - White bloom at clamp foot
A pale oxide bloom around a clamp foot means the coating is sacrificing itself.
Clean, measure coating loss, and retorque after the surface is dry.

## Card 03 - Hidden washer stack
Two washers under one bolt can mask thread engagement. The joint may pass a
visual check and still lose preload during wind vibration.

## Card 04 - FRP edge bruise
FRP supports can hide impact damage under a glossy edge. Tap testing reveals a
dull patch where fibers separated below the surface.

## Card 05 - Galvanized cut end
Hot-dip galvanized members need sealed cut ends. A bright saw mark near salty
air becomes the first red rust point.

## Card 06 - Dripline shadow
Rows below a cable dripline collect concentrated runoff. Staining here often
appears before the rest of the array shows distress.

## Card 07 - Torque after fog
Do not complete torque audit during morning fog. Wet threads change friction and
produce misleading readings.

## Card 08 - Isolator creep
Polymer isolators can creep under heat. A joint that was electrically separated
in winter may touch metal-to-metal by late summer.

## Card 09 - Mixed metal bracket
Aluminum brackets bolted to stainless hardware need isolation. Without it, the
less noble metal becomes the damage site.

## Card 10 - Drain notch blocked
A blocked drain notch turns a channel into a trough. Standing water accelerates
coating failure and dirt packing.

## Card 11 - Bird screen overtightened
Bird screen clips should be snug, not crushing. Overtightening dents the module
frame and creates a local stress riser.

## Card 12 - Cable tie memory
UV-aged cable ties keep their old bend even after replacement. Check whether the
cable jacket has already been notched.

## Card 13 - Shift handoff drift
Repair quality falls when the outgoing crew writes "tightened" without recording
which row, tool, and torque value were used. Require row-level evidence.

## Card 14 - Salt pan reflection
Sites near evaporation ponds receive chloride from air and reflected heat from
the pan. Treat them as harsher than ordinary coastal locations.

## Card 15 - Anchor grout pocket
A void in anchor grout stores water beside the stud. The top surface may look
sealed while the pocket works below the plate.

## Card 16 - Wind rattle witness
A faint rattle during gusts is a witness signal. Look for ovalized holes before
replacing parts.

## Card 17 - HDG thickness record
For hot-dip galvanized steel, keep coating thickness readings by batch. A single
thin batch can explain scattered failures.

## Card 18 - FRP ultraviolet chalk
Chalking on FRP is not automatically structural failure. Pair visual whitening
with stiffness or tap evidence before replacement.

## Card 19 - Module frame bite
Clamp jaws that bite the module frame leave a crescent mark. This indicates the
load path shifted from friction to local indentation.

## Card 20 - Access mat omission
Technicians stepping directly on ballast trays bend lips and change drainage.
Use access mats where repeated inspections occur.

## Card 21 - Label lost in sun
Sun-faded labels break maintenance traceability. Photograph serial labels during
commissioning and store the images with row IDs.

## Card 22 - Edge row uplift
Edge rows receive stronger wind demand. Do not copy center-row fastener spacing
to the perimeter without checking the design note.

## Card 23 - Sealant bridge trap
Sealant bridging across a lap joint traps moisture. Leave a drain path unless
the detail explicitly calls for full encapsulation.

## Card 24 - Cleaning chemical residue
Some detergents leave conductive residue. After module washing, rinse the support
rail where overspray collected.

## Card 25 - Cable tray galvanic path
A cable tray can create an unexpected electrical path between isolated metals.
Check bonding after tray modifications.

## Card 26 - Torque wrench calendar
Calibration certificates are not enough. The wrench calendar must match the tool
actually used on the row.

## Card 27 - Reused locknut
Reused locknuts lose prevailing torque. Replace them after removal in coastal
service.

## Card 28 - Sand pack under base
Wind-blown sand under a base plate holds moisture. Vacuum before reseating the
plate.

## Card 29 - Thermal bow
Long rails bow during afternoon heat. Leave expansion gaps clear of sealant,
cable ties, and debris.

## Card 30 - Missing photo angle
A close-up photo without row context is weak evidence. Capture the defect, the
row marker, and the neighboring module in one set.

## Card 31 - Near-road soot
Road soot mixed with marine aerosol forms sticky grime. It hides early coating
loss better than clean salt crystals.

## Card 32 - Crane pick scar
Temporary crane picks can nick a member before installation. Inspect lifting
points, not only installed joints.

## Card 33 - Ledger ghost
When a defect is fixed but the work order stays open, later crews repeat the
inspection and skip new issues. Close the record with evidence.

## Card 34 - Inverter heat plume
Supports behind inverter exhaust age faster. Heat plume exposure changes polymer
isolator life.

## Card 35 - Foundation splash zone
The first 300 millimeters above grade sees splash, fertilizer, and mud. Treat it
as a separate exposure zone.

## Card 36 - Stainless tea stain
Brown staining on stainless is often surface contamination, not deep attack.
Clean and verify before escalating to replacement.

## Card 37 - Missing isolation sleeve
A bolt isolation sleeve can slip out during assembly. The washer still appears
correct while the shank touches the bracket.

## Card 38 - Night condensation check
Condensation patterns reveal cold bridges. Inspect at dawn to find members that
stay wet longer than their neighbors.

## Card 39 - Overwide slot
An overwide slotted hole allows alignment but reduces bearing area. Add the
specified plate washer, not an improvised stack.

## Card 40 - Paint over rust
Paint over active rust creates a tidy surface and a bad repair. Remove corrosion
products before coating.

## Card 41 - Spare part mismatch
A spare part with the same length can have a different alloy. Verify material
marking, not just geometry.

## Card 42 - Array corner shortcut
Crews often stage tools at array corners. Repeated foot traffic loosens ballast
and damages cable protection.

## Card 43 - Weep hole orientation
Weep holes must face down after installation. A rotated member stores water even
when the drawing is correct.

## Card 44 - Salt spray test caveat
ASTM B117 salt spray data is comparative, not a complete service-life prediction.
Use it with field exposure notes.

## Card 45 - Z275 coating limit
Z275 sheet coating is not a universal marine answer. Check cut edges, scratches,
and maintenance interval before accepting it near chloride sources.

## Card 46 - FRP drilling dust
FRP drilling dust irritates skin and lungs. Use extraction and seal the hole
edge after drilling.

## Card 47 - Emergency repair debt
Temporary clamps installed after a storm need expiry dates. Without a date, they
become permanent weak details.

## Card 48 - Ground lug looseness
Ground lugs loosen when paint thickness prevents full seating. Remove coating
only where the bonding detail requires contact.

## Card 49 - Procurement substitution
A cheaper bracket may meet strength but fail durability. Procurement approvals
must include exposure class and material finish.

## Card 50 - Evidence bundle
A good support defect report includes row ID, weather, close photo, context
photo, torque value, coating reading, and the repair decision.
